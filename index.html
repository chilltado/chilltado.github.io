THE DESIGN PROBLEM

Idle games rarely have stakes: progress only accumulates, so nothing that happens while you are away can matter. BotRaid puts the idle loop on contested ground instead. Platforms dock edge to edge, each with a core at its middle that builds robots forever. Whoever has an army standing on a platform owns its core — take the ground and the core comes with it, lose the ground and it goes with it. The war runs continuously, including while the app is closed.

  • 01

    NO BUILD PHASE

    No battle phase, no wave counter, no start button. One continuous war you are always in the middle of.

  • 02

    A PLATFORM EVERY 45s

    A hostile grid docks onto your territory on a clock — and you can call the next one in early when you want the fight now.

  • 03

    CORES CHANGE HANDS

    Hold enemy ground for 8 seconds and the core flips, along with everything it produces. Your home platform is the one thing that can never be taken.

  • 04

    PARTS BECOME ROBOTS

    Wrecked robots drop parts and parts become robots, so the army in front of you decides the army behind you.

  • 05

    BEHAVIOUR YOU SEE

    A mortar arcs over the line, a grappler drags a target off its tile, a mine trips on contact, a kamikaze runs in on a fuse. Nothing is a hidden multiplier.

  • 06

    COMMAND, NOT MICRO

    Robots pick their own targets. You decide what gets built, where it stands and where the line moves next — and you can pause the whole war to think.

CASE STUDY · IN PRODUCTION

A hoverboard racer with the movement of Sonic Riders and the mini-turbo of Mario Kart, in 1–2 minute races where the community builds the tracks.

ROLESolo developer
GENREArcade racer
ENGINEUnity 6 · URP
PLATFORMAndroid, portrait
FORMAT3 laps, 6 riders

DESIGN PILLARS

  • 01

    A RACE FITS IN A BREAK

    60 to 120 seconds from tap to result. Every other system in the game is budgeted against that number.

  • 02

    SPEED IS EARNED

    Drift, trick, clean landing, rail. There is no passive boost — the boost bar is a scoreboard for execution.

  • 03

    COMMUNITY = CONTENT

    The track editor is a game mode, not an extra feature. The game does not depend on one person authoring tracks forever.

  • 04

    ASYNC, NOT REAL TIME

    Opponents are replays of real players rendered as ordinary riders. No real-time netcode and no racing AI to write.

CONCEPT

Two pieces from the ongoing production — the main menu and the gameplay concept. Not captures of the running build.

CONTROL

The board moves forward on its own — no input is needed to travel. A slow lateral drag steers; a fast one starts a drift; dragging back brakes and charges a jump whose height is proportional to the drag when you release. Low skill floor, high ceiling.

The character is purely cosmetic — every stat comes from the board. Ranked runs on normalised specs, so upgrades never buy time on the leaderboard.

  • C#
  • Config → Controller → Renderer
  • Offline-first
  • Replays as opponents
  • UGC track editor
  • Go backend

CASE STUDY · SHIPPED

Packet

A 3D delivery platformer where the levels are the content: run the route, or build your own, clear it yourself, and publish it for everyone else to run.

GOOGLE PLAY
GENRE3D platformer maker
PLATFORMAndroid
CONTENTPlayer-built routes
SCORINGPer-level leaderboards

VIDEO

BUILD, CLEAR, PUBLISH

A level only reaches other players once its author has cleared it themselves — which keeps the published set playable without anyone moderating it by hand. Every route is scored, so the same level is worth running again for a better time.

BUILT WITH

Player-built levels are uploaded to object storage and served back through a CDN, behind a Go service running on EC2 from an ECR image — so a published route reaches other players without shipping a build. On the client, movement is physics-driven and playable on a gamepad or on touch, with the interface in UI Toolkit.

  • C#
  • UI Toolkit
  • Physics-driven movement
  • Gamepad + touch controls

  • S3 + CDN
  • User-generated levels

Raise a pet, feed it, wash it and take it to town. At this size the interface has to be one decision per screen: the map is the navigation, icons are the labels, and nothing depends on text a young player has to read.

SPACEQUIZZ

A science game for the same watch: quiz rounds, ordering the planets by hand, naming moons and tracing constellations star by star. Recorded on a phone and squashed back to the watch’s 184×224 proportions.

CAPRICORNUS

  • 01

    DRAG, NOT TYPE

    Every answer is a drag or a tap on the thing itself — planets into slots, a line between stars — because a watch has no keyboard and a kid has no patience for one.

  • 02

    TIMED ROUNDS

    Each round runs against a clock with a score, so replaying the same content has a reason to happen.

  • 03

    REAL CONTENT

    Actual moons, actual constellations. The game teaches names that exist, which is the whole point of putting it on a kid's wrist.
