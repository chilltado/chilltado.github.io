The idea

Each solid unfolds into its net where you drop it. A face that lands on solid ground becomes floor; a face that hits a wall stands up as a wall, or as a stair to the floor above. Rotate the solid in the tray first and the same piece becomes a bridge, a barrier or a step.

  • 01

    Every level checked

    A solver replays every level through the game's own rules on each build, and refuses the build if any level can't be won with the solids in its tray.

  • 02

    Nothing is decoration

    Remove a key, a switch or a spring from a level and it must stop being solvable. If it doesn't, the level is regenerated.

  • 03

    A jigsaw per world

    Each level hides one piece of the world's illustration. Finding them all is the world's achievement.

The design problem

The first BotRaid was an idle war on endlessly docking platforms. It kept the player waiting and gave them nothing to decide, so it was rebuilt as a puzzle: one board, one enemy formation, one question. The energy bar does not move until you press FIGHT, and after that it never stops, so the press is not the end of your turn. A board is won by occupation — stand on ground the enemy is not standing on and it changes hands.

  • 01

    A campaign of boards

    Every board is its own question about one formation. Gold is the only thing carried between boards.

  • 02

    Three stars, three families

    One star each for time, survival and restraint, so no single overwhelming force answers all three.

  • 03

    Ground, not kill counts

    Ground is claimed grid by grid on both sides. Take an enemy grid outright and it changes sides; lose your last one and the board is over.

  • 04

    An opponent that plays

    No waves. The enemy adds robots through the fight on its own energy, like a second player.

  • 05

    Parts with rarity

    Fit parts to a chassis, from Standard to Legendary. Duplicates level them, and what you fit is charged against your energy ceiling.

  • 06

    The tutorial is chapter zero

    Three boards that can only be won by doing the thing they teach, each with one line over it. Checked by simulation over 2000 seeds.

Build, clear, publish

A level only reaches other players once its author has cleared it themselves — which keeps the published set playable without anyone moderating it by hand. Every route is scored, so the same level is worth running again for a better time.

Built with

Player-built levels are uploaded to object storage and served back through a CDN, behind a Go service running on EC2 from an ECR image — so a published route reaches other players without shipping a build. On the client, movement is physics-driven and playable on a gamepad or on touch, with the interface in UI Toolkit.

Tamagotchi

Raise a pet, feed it, wash it and take it to town. At this size the interface has to be one decision per screen: the map is the navigation, icons are the labels, and nothing depends on text a young player has to read.

  • 01

    Care

    Feeding, washing and play keep the pet's state up, each through its own short mini-game rather than a menu.

  • 02

    Town

    A map screen turns navigation into a place: home, shop and activities are markers a child taps, not a list.

  • 03

    Collect

    An inventory of items and outfits gives the loop something to spend on and something to come back for.

SpaceQuizz

A science game for the same watch: quiz rounds, ordering the planets by hand, naming moons and tracing constellations star by star. Recorded on a phone and squashed back to the watch’s 184×224 proportions.

CAPRICORNUS

  • 01

    Drag, not type

    Every answer is a drag or a tap on the thing itself — planets into slots, a line between stars — because a watch has no keyboard and a kid has no patience for one.

  • 02

    Timed rounds

    Each round runs against a clock with a score, so replaying the same content has a reason to happen.

  • 03

    Real content

    Actual moons, actual constellations. The game teaches names that exist, which is the whole point of putting it on a kid's wrist.

ABOUT

I make games from first prototype to release.

Gameplay systems, tools, backend, storefront and whatever else shipping asks for. My work has been mobile so far, but the part I actually do — gameplay systems, tools, backend and getting a build out the door — is not platform-specific, and I am open to any opportunity in games.

I work in Unity 6 with URP and C#, on an architecture where every feature owns exactly one concern. That is what makes it possible to keep several projects running in parallel without one becoming the other's debt.

BotRaid is live on Google Play, localised into 12 languages, with cloud save, weekly leaderboards and full offline progression.
